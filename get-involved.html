01 · Volunteer

Give a Saturday. Or a skill. Or a lift.

Volunteering with SYLI is practical. You might help run a workshop station, take photos at a clean-up, translate a session into Chichewa, or drive materials to a school. We will not invent a role for you to look busy.

What we ask: be on time, respect safeguarding, and tell us early if you cannot make it. What you get: real experience, a reference if you need one, and a group of people who will remember you showed up.

  • One-off events or a regular monthly commitment
  • In Salima or remote (design, translation, social media, admin)
  • Students, professionals, retirees and members of the diaspora all welcome

02 · Mentor

One consistent adult can change a whole trajectory.

Our Mentorship Circles pair six to ten young people with someone from the district who has walked a road they want to walk: a health worker, a teacher, a farmer, a business owner, a graduate.

You do not need a degree in mentoring. You need to listen, keep your word, and be willing to say “I don't know, let's find out.” We will brief you on safeguarding and give you a simple structure for each meeting.

  • A few hours a month for at least six months
  • Based in or near Salima, or meeting online where possible
  • Comfortable talking about money, failure and choices honestly

03 · Partner

Work with us, not around us.

The best partnerships are specific. Tell us what you actually have — a room, a bus, a facilitator, a market — and we will tell you honestly whether it fits.

Schools & colleges

Host a career talk or leadership session. We bring young facilitators; you bring the learners and an honest conversation.

Clinics & health centres

Co-design health and safeguarding sessions, refer young people safely, and share accurate information instead of rumour.

Businesses & farms

Offer a placement, buy a harvest at a fair price, lend equipment, or send someone to teach a practical skill.

Community & traditional leaders

Help us reach the young people who are not in any club or group — and make sure our work fits the community's own priorities.

Fellow NGOs

Share tools, refer participants, co-facilitate, or simply tell us what you learned the hard way. We would rather collaborate than duplicate.

Funders & institutions

Fund a pilot with realistic overheads and honest reporting. We will show you what worked and what did not.

04 · Support

What a small, specific gift actually does

We would rather tell you exactly what something costs than ask for a vague donation. These are real line items from our work.

Transport

Bring a girl to a session and home again

Bus fare is the quiet reason many young women miss trainings. Covering it changes who is in the room.

Materials

Printing, flip charts, pens and a room

The unglamorous basics of a workshop. Small, and completely necessary.

Seed & inputs

A first chilli plot for a youth group

Seed, basic inputs and a share of the drying costs — paired with record-keeping coaching so the harvest teaches something.

Equipment

A camera, a laptop or a projector

So members can document their own work, write their own stories and run their own sessions.

Time

Professional advice

An hour of accounting, safeguarding, legal or communications advice is worth more than it sounds.

Please ask before sending money. The website does not publish payment details yet. Message us first and we will confirm the correct, current account and what your support will be used for. Never send money to an account you have not verified with a named member of the team.

What happens next

No black hole. Here is the process.

We are a small team, so we will not pretend to answer instantly. But we will answer.

1. You write

Use the contact form or Messenger and tell us what you can offer and how much time you have.

2. We reply

A named member of the team responds, usually within a few days, with an honest yes, no or “not yet”.

3. We meet

A short call or in-person chat to check fit, safeguarding and expectations — both ways.

4. We start small

A first session or a small piece of support. If it works, we build on it. If not, we say so.

Still deciding?

Ask us the awkward question.

How is money handled? Who decides? What happens if a volunteer is not a good fit? Ask. A organisation worth joining can answer.
